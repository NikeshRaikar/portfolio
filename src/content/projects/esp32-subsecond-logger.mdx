## Why Linux Isn't Always Fast Enough

Even an aggressively stripped Linux image on a single-board computer takes several seconds to reach userspace and initialize `SocketCAN`. During automotive cold-crank or wake-up testing, critical network management (NM) frames occur within the **first 200–500 milliseconds**.

## Hardware & Firmware Approach

* **Controller:** Waveshare **ESP32-S3 ETH** board combining wired Ethernet with dual-core FreeRTOS execution.
* **Transceiver:** **SN65HVD230** 3.3V CAN transceiver wired to the ESP32-S3 TWAI peripheral.
* **Real-Time Pipeline:** Dedicated FreeRTOS task pinned to Core 1 drains the CAN RX FIFO immediately on boot, buffering wake-up frames in PSRAM before flushing over Ethernet once the link negotiates.

## Key Takeaway

Pairing a sub-second microcontroller front-end with a Linux companion logger solves the classic cold-boot blind spot in automotive bench diagnostics.
